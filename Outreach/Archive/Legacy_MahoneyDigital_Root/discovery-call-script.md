# Discovery Call Script

A structured 30-minute framework for first conversations with prospects. The goal is to understand their situation, qualify the fit, and leave with a clear next step — not to sell on the call.

**Before the call:** Review their current website (if any), Google Business listing, and social presence. Note 2–3 specific observations.

---

## Opening (2 minutes)

> "Thanks for making time, [Name]. I'm Jeremy with Mahoney Digital — I help local businesses in Chillicothe and Ross County get more from their websites. This call is just to understand where you're at and whether I can help. No pitch, no pressure. Sound good?"

**Set the frame:**
- 30 minutes
- You'll ask questions first, then share initial thoughts
- They'll leave knowing whether a proposal makes sense

---

## Business Context (5 minutes)

Ask and listen. Take notes.

1. **"Tell me about [Business Name] — what do you do, and who are your typical customers?"**
2. **"How do most customers find you today?"** (word of mouth, Google, social, drive-by, etc.)
3. **"What's working well right now? What's frustrating?"**
4. **"Are you the decision-maker on website and marketing, or is someone else involved?"**

*Listen for:* Revenue goals, seasonality, competition, decision-making process.

---

## Current Web Presence (5 minutes)

1. **"Walk me through your current website — when did it last get updated?"**
2. **"Do you get leads, calls, or bookings from it? Roughly how many per month?"**
3. **"How do you feel when you look at your site or send someone the link?"**
4. **"Do you manage it yourself, or does someone else handle updates?"**
5. **"Is your Google Business Profile set up? Are you showing up in local searches?"**

*Listen for:* Pain points (outdated, slow, embarrassing, no mobile, no leads), DIY fatigue, previous bad agency experience.

---

## Goals & Timeline (5 minutes)

1. **"If we fast-forward six months and your online presence is exactly where you want it — what does that look like?"**
2. **"What's driving the timing? Is there a deadline or event coming up?"**
3. **"Have you budgeted for this, or are you still figuring out what it should cost?"**
4. **"Have you talked to anyone else about this, or gotten other quotes?"**

*Listen for:* Urgency, budget range, competing quotes, realistic expectations.

---

## Qualification Check (internal — don't read aloud)

Mark mentally or in notes:

| Signal | Good Fit | Poor Fit |
|--------|----------|----------|
| Budget | $1,200+ for a project, or $75+/mo for care | Expects free work or sub-$500 |
| Timeline | Ready to start within 4–8 weeks | "Someday" with no urgency |
| Decision | They can say yes, or partner is on a follow-up call | Committee of 5+ with no authority |
| Goals | More leads, credibility, local visibility | Vanity redesign with no business case |
| Content | Can provide photos and text (or budget for help) | Expects you to invent their entire business |

---

## Share Initial Thoughts (5 minutes)

Based on what you heard, offer 2–3 specific observations. Be direct but not harsh.

**Template:**

> "Based on what you've shared, here's what I'm seeing:
>
> 1. [Specific observation — e.g., 'Your site loads slowly on mobile, which hurts Google rankings.']
> 2. [Specific observation — e.g., 'You don't have service pages, so you're missing local search traffic.']
> 3. [Specific observation — e.g., 'Your Google listing has no photos — competitors with photos get more clicks.']
>
> I think a [Starter / Growth / Custom] build would fit what you're describing. Ballpark, that's around [$X]. I can send a written proposal with exact scope and timeline within [2–3 business days]."

**If not a fit:**

> "Honestly, I don't think I'm the right fit for this — [reason: budget, timeline, scope outside your services]. I'd recommend [alternative: DIY tool, different provider, wait until X]. Happy to point you in the right direction."

---

## Close & Next Steps (3 minutes)

**If proceeding:**

> "I'll send a proposal to [email] by [date]. It'll include scope, timeline, price, and payment terms. If it looks right, we schedule a kickoff call and get started. Any questions before we wrap?"

**Confirm:**
- Best email for the proposal
- Anyone else who needs to review it
- Preferred follow-up method (email, call, text)

**If they need to think:**

> "No rush. I'll send the proposal anyway so you have something concrete to review. If questions come up, reach me at [email] or @Biggs237 on X. Does [date] work for a quick follow-up check-in?"

## Post-Call Checklist

Within 24 hours:

- [ ] Log the lead in [leads/leads-tracker.md](../leads/leads-tracker.md)
- [ ] Send a brief thank-you email (even if not proceeding)
- [ ] Draft proposal if qualified (due within 2–3 business days)
- [ ] Set a follow-up reminder if no response in 5 business days

## Quick Reference — Objection Responses

**"That seems expensive."**
> "I hear you. The question is what a new customer is worth to you. If the site brings one extra job a month, it pays for itself. I can also break it into a deposit and launch payment, or start with a smaller Starter build."

**"My nephew can do it cheaper."**
> "That might work if he has time and you don't need it done soon. Most of my clients come to me after a DIY or family project stalled. If you go that route, happy to do a quick audit afterward — no charge."

**"I need to think about it."**
> "Of course. I'll send the proposal so you have the details in writing. What's the main thing you want to think through — budget, timing, or whether this is the right approach?"

**"Can you just fix a few things on my current site?"**
> "Depends on what's broken. I offer on-demand work at $85/hour, or a monthly care plan if you need ongoing help. Let's look at the site together and I'll tell you honestly whether a patch or a rebuild makes more sense."
